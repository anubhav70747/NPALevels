# Finite exact depth, unbounded depth near a boundary, and the remaining coverage conjecture

Anubhav Chaturvedi

Every fixed full(2,2,2) Bell objective has a finite exact standard NPA level, by a recent perfect Positivstellensatz for its **same universal observable algebra**. Nevertheless these finite depths are unbounded near every positive doubly tilted critical-edge point. The edge point itself is exact already at1+AB. The apparent tension disappears once pointwise depth and depth along an approaching sequence are distinguished.

This note concerns support gaps in **Bell-direction space**, not arbitrary boundary points of the quantum behavior set. Source details and inspected theorem scopes are in `SOURCES.md`.

## 1. Standard exact depth and its topology

For the eight real Bell coefficients b, let h_b be the corresponding marginal-plus-correlator polynomial, with no identity term. Let W_k contain all reduced reflection words of total length at most k, and let h_k(b) be the standard universal NPA support bound. Write h_Q(b) for the quantum support. Define

\[
g_k(b)=h_k(b)-h_Q(b)\geq0,\quad
A_k=\{b:g_k(b)=0\},\quad D_k=\{b:g_k(b)>0\},
\]

and

\[
d(b)=\min\{k\geq1:b\in A_k\}.
\]

An infinite value would mean that one fixed objective misses its quantum optimum at every finite standard level. Section2 proves that this does not occur in this scenario.

All supports are continuous: their retained physical coordinates lie in[-1,1], so each support is Lipschitz with constant1 in the coefficient L1 norm. Therefore A_k is closed, D_k is open, and

\[
A_k\subseteq A_{k+1},\qquad D_{k+1}\subseteq D_k.
\]

The depth d is lower semicontinuous, since {d>k}=D_k. Adding the intermediate list W_(1+AB) gives the same statements for its admission and failure sets, denoted A_(1+AB),D_(1+AB).

**Boundary theorem.** Every point on the ambient boundary of D_k is admitted at level k. The same holds on the boundary of any path component of D_k, and for1+AB.

Indeed the boundary of an open strict-gap set has g_k=0. A component's boundary cannot lie inside D_k: any sufficiently small ball inside D_k is connected, so if it meets that component it belongs to the same component and makes the point interior. Thus a boundary point of a1+AB failure component has integer exact depth at most2. It cannot itself have infinite depth.

This is an exact refutation of the interpretation that points on the boundary of the1+AB support-gap domain require infinitely many levels. An artificial chart cut, such as imposing a>=b, is not such a boundary and must not be substituted for it.

## 2. A primary theorem proves finite standard depth for every fixed objective

[Dritschel–Klep–McCullough–Volčič, arXiv:2608.23073v1, Corollary4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23073v1#S4), submitted24August2026, proves that every positive semidefinite element of

\[
\mathcal A_{2,2}=\mathbb C[(\mathbb Z_2*\mathbb Z_2)\times(\mathbb Z_2*\mathbb Z_2)]
\]

is a finite sum of hermitian squares **inside this algebra**. This is precisely the universal algebra generated by A0,A1,B0,B1 with reflection and opposite-party commutation relations. It supplies the standard-word transfer absent from a bare commutative angle reduction.

Here is an explicit application bridge avoiding an unspecified commuting-versus-tensor assumption. Put R=complex Laurent polynomials in z,w, with z*=z^-1,w*=w^-1. The regular finite-crossed-product embedding uses local matrices

\[
U_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U_1(z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&z\\z^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

and sends the four generators to U0 tensor I,U1(z) tensor I,I tensor U0,I tensor U1(w). This is an injective universal-algebra map into M4(R): the rotation subgroup maps to diagonal Laurent monomials and the two reflections supply the independent finite-group cosets.

For |z|=|w|=1 these are actual two-qubit local Hermitian reflections. Consequently the matrix polynomial image of p=h_Q(b)I-h_b is positive semidefinite at every torus point, by the definition of the quantum maximum. Corollary4.1 of the cited paper factors that matrix polynomial as B*B with finite Laurent-polynomial entries. Its proof of Corollary4.2 uses a strong conditional expectation from M4(R) onto the embedded algebra that preserves sums of hermitian squares. Applying it returns a finite hermitian-square identity for p in the original universal algebra.

Every square factor has finite support in reduced words. Choose k containing all those supports. The identity gives h_k(b)<=h_Q(b), and the physical inclusion gives the reverse inequality. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\quad \forall b\in\mathbb R^8\ \exists k<\infty:
h_k(b)=h_Q(b),\qquad d(b)<\infty.\quad}
\]

Complex coefficients are harmless. Write each factor as u_i+i*v_i with u_i,v_i in the real group algebra. Coefficientwise conjugation fixes p and changes the sign of the imaginary cross terms. Averaging the identity and its conjugate gives p=sum_i(u_i^*u_i+v_i^*v_i), a real standard-word SOS. Equivalently the complex Gram may be averaged with its conjugate. No qubit identities are added to the universal coefficient equations. The source theorem accepts semidefinite slack, including its optimal zeros; neither a strict-positivity margin nor rational factors with unresolved denominators remain in this transfer.

This proves finite exactness of the **support value** for each fixed noncritical doubly tilted objective, and in fact for every fixed full(2,2,2) objective. It provides no uniform k over directions and does not compute the first exact level. Equality of one support value does not by itself prove finite recovery of its full optimal behavior face, or one level that captures all supporting directions at a fixed behavior. The earlier audited commutative-to-NPA argument is not used or repaired by assertion; this separate algebra theorem supplies the missing bridge.

## 3. Global depth accumulation has a precise closed locus

The admission sets now satisfy union_k A_k=R8, while every D_k is nonempty by the fixed-level Motzkin obstruction. Define

\[
\mathcal U=\bigcap_{k\geq1}\overline{D_k}
=\mathbb R^8\setminus\bigcup_{k\geq1}\operatorname{int}A_k.
\]

This is exactly the locus where the finite-valued depth is **not locally bounded**. It is closed and nowhere dense. For density of its complement, apply the Baire category theorem to the nested closed cover A_k in any open coefficient ball: some A_k contains a nonempty open subregion there. Thus finite uniform-depth neighborhoods form an open dense set. This is a topological statement, not a Zariski-generic assertion.

Every u in U still has a finite pointwise depth m=d(u). For all k>=m it lies on the boundary of D_k, because it belongs to A_k and to closure(D_k). In contrast,

\[
\bigcap_kD_k=\varnothing,
\qquad \bigcap_k\overline{D_k}=\mathcal U\ne\varnothing.
\]

Interchanging failure sets with their closures would incorrectly turn arbitrarily large finite depth into a fixed infinite-depth objective.

All statements may be restricted to a normalized direction sphere, for example sum|b_i|=1. Positive scaling leaves depth unchanged; the zero objective is omitted from that normalized space and has depth1 if kept in R8. The normalized sphere is compact and Baire. Every compact subset outside U has a uniform finite depth bound, by a finite subcover of bounded-depth neighborhoods. Any sequence of normalized objectives with depths tending to infinity has a subsequence converging to U. Conversely a singleton in U still has finite depth, so merely meeting U does not make every compact set unbounded in depth.

The128 physical relabelings preserve the standard word lists, supports and depth. These results therefore descend to the finite symmetry quotient, including the Bell cost simplex with its H16 residual action.

## 4. Every positive critical-edge point is a common finite-level failure boundary

Fix a0,b0>0,a0+b0=2, and define

\[
b(\tau):\quad a=a_0-\tau/2,\quad b=b_0-\tau/2,
\qquad0<\tau<2\min(a_0,b_0).
\]

The independently proved asymmetric divergence theorem gives, for every fixed k,

\[
\frac{g_k(b(\tau))}{\tau^3}\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{\ \forall K\in\mathbb N\ \exists\varepsilon_K>0\
\forall\tau\in(0,\varepsilon_K):\ d(b(\tau))>K.\ }
\]

Each depth in this formula is finite by Section2. The thresholds can shrink with K. No rate or one common interval for every level is implied. In sequence form, every tau_n↓0 along this normal has d(b(tau_n))→infinity.

At tau=0, however, the universal event identity is

\[
4I-H_{a_0,b_0}=2b_0P(+,-|00)+2a_0P(-,+|00)
+4P(-,+|01)+4P(+,-|10)+4P(-,-|11).
\]

Every event projector is its own square and is a polynomial in W_(1+AB). The all-positive deterministic behavior attains4, so the endpoint is admitted at1+AB.

Under the **raw observable** W1={I,A0,A1,B0,B1} convention, this endpoint has integer depth exactly2. A feasible rank-two Gram uses vI=vA0=vB0=e0 and vA1=e0/2+sqrt3*e1/2,vB1=e0/2-sqrt3*e1/2. It attains a0+b0+5/2=9/2>4. Thus raw W1 fails while1+AB succeeds. This Gram has a reconstructed probability-1/8; adding probability positivity changes that level-one convention. With such strengthening, the positive-event inequality already gives the endpoint bound directly.

For every integer k>=2, the endpoint is in A_k and is approached by points of D_k. It is therefore a common boundary point of all D_k, k>=2, and also of D_(1+AB). Its depth is2, not infinity. Every positive critical-edge point belongs to U.

The profile is directional. On the critical/supercritical side a,b>=0,a+b>=2, rescaling the event identity and adding positive multiples of I-A0,I-B0 proves1+AB exactness. The same raw-W1 Gram still exceeds the local value2+a+b by1/2, so integer depth is2 throughout that positive supercritical region. Hence inward normals have unbounded finite depth, while outward approaches keep depth2. The claim that every approach to this boundary has diverging depth is false.

The compact-edge extension in `analysis/connectivity/CONNECTIVITY_REDUCTION.md` makes the inward divergence uniform on each compact positive-edge interval for a fixed k. Its overlapping failure collars form one connected1+AB failure family accumulating on the entire positive edge. This does not prove that every other failure component joins it.

## 5. What remains of the proposed universal boundary conjecture

Let C be one component of D_(1+AB) in normalized Bell-direction space. The statement that its boundary points themselves have infinite exact depth is false: all those points are admitted at1+AB, and indeed all objectives have finite depth.

The meaningful stronger question is whether C contains an approach to its boundary with depths tending to infinity. Define

\[
\mathcal U_C=\bigcap_k\overline{C\cap D_k}.
\]

The accumulation points of unbounded depth **within C** are exactly U_C. A boundary version requires U_C intersect boundary(C) to be nonempty; a path version additionally asks for a continuous curve inside C realizing that approach. Merely having closure(C) intersect U is weaker, because nearby high-depth directions could belong to different components. U_C can also contain internal points of C whose own depth is finite but greater than2.

For the component containing the positive-edge Motzkin collar, the required boundary approach is proved. For every excluded component modulo all relabelings, it is not yet proved. Connectivity of the full failure set and coverage of its depth-accumulation behavior remain separate from the pointwise finite-depth theorem.

No conclusion follows that every boundary of every low-level admission patch has depth blowup. A regular finite-level transition may have a bounded-depth neighborhood; the exact set U identifies the additional property that must be established.

## 6. A terminating exact level search for algebraic Bell input

For b with exact real-algebraic coordinates, the following is a decision algorithm, although no efficient complexity or useful level bound is claimed.

1. Compute h_Q(b) exactly by real-closed-field quantifier elimination on the compact real-qubit atom set. A linear Bell maximum over its convex hull equals the maximum over a single atom. The objective and compact polynomial constraints have algebraic coefficients, so the attained value is a real algebraic number, representable by a polynomial and isolating interval.
2. For k=1,2,... construct W_k and test whether h_Q(b)I-h_b=w*Qw for some real Q>=0. Expand all universal ordered-word coefficients. PSD is a finite semialgebraic condition, expressible by principal minors or a real Gram factorization. Exact real quantifier elimination decides this finite problem.
3. Stop at the first feasible k and return it, together with an algebraic Gram witness. Algebraic solutions exist whenever the algebraic-coefficient semialgebraic feasibility problem has a real solution, by real-closed-field transfer. Section2 proves termination.

One may insert1+AB between integer levels1 and2 if the first exact level in that convention is desired. This algorithm has not been run as a general classifier here. It depends on the exact optimum and exact universal SOS equations; a floating solver match is not the stopping certificate.
